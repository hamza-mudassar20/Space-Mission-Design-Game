## 1) Project Summary — ~145 words
**CYGNOVA Mission Forge** turns space mission design into a playable systems-engineering challenge. Players plan Earth, lunar or Martian science missions, select orbits and launch systems, equip spacecraft with instruments, and negotiate the competing constraints of mass, budget, available power and data transmission. Every choice produces immediate engineering feedback. When the spacecraft launches, mission operators encounter two unexpected events and must trade scientific opportunity against system safety. The final score and engineering debrief reveal why the design succeeded or failed and encourage players to redesign and compare alternatives. The game includes an optional same-device four-role council, three difficulty settings, downloadable mission reports and referenced NASA orbit examples: Landsat 9, Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter and Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter. We built an accessible, self-contained browser experience requiring no account or installation. CYGNOVA teaches a key truth of space engineering: a mission succeeds when every subsystem works together.

## 4) Project Details
CYGNOVA is a single-page, cross-platform educational strategy game built in HTML5, CSS3 and vanilla JavaScript. Players select one of three science missions (Earth climate monitoring, lunar polar ice mapping and Mars mineral/atmosphere reconnaissance). They then configure a spacecraft bus, solar system, communications hardware, shielding, scientific instrument suite, launch vehicle, orbital strategy and mission lifetime. Every configuration is evaluated using a deliberately simplified, documented mission-system model. The interface shows cost, mass, available electrical generation, subsystem power needs and science data versus downlink capacity. A rule-based advisor teaches why a choice has consequences. An eight-step mission story introduces two interactive emergencies. Players choose whether to reduce risk or preserve science output and receive a 0–100 report with quantitative feedback and engineering lessons. Optional same-device hot-seat role approvals support up to four contributors; the game does not claim networked multiplayer. Users can export a JSON blueprint, download postmission results and compare redesigns. The game uses NASA-referenced mission altitudes for Landsat 9 (705 km), LRO (50 km), and MRO imaging (~300 km), linked in the interface. All other equipment performance values and science-score coefficients are fictional teaching parameters, not real NASA mission-planning data. The project contains no NASA marks, logos or mission badges and runs without third-party dependencies.
